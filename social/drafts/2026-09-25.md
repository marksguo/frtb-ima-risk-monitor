Weekly FRTB IMA Risk Desk Recap | Week Ending September 25, 2026

Our 97.5% Expected Shortfall for today stands at 0.0174. This is the core measure of potential loss under normal market conditions. The stressed Expected Shortfall, at 0.0256, indicates a significant increase in potential tail risk during periods of market stress. The liquidity-adjusted ES, which adds extra holding-period penalties for assets that are harder to sell quickly, is even higher at 0.0365. The substantial gap between the normal and liquidity-adjusted figures suggests that illiquidity risk is a major contributor to our overall capital charge right now.

The current volatility regime is elevated. This implies that the market is experiencing higher than usual price fluctuations, which naturally drives up risk metrics. It also means our models are working with a richer dataset of recent movements, which should lead to more accurate, albeit higher, risk estimates.

The biggest mover today was EEM, up 1.085%. This emerging markets ETF has been a consistent contributor to daily PnL changes, and its volatility often impacts our overall ES calculation. Its positive movement today partially offsets other potential losses, but also reflects the broader market's current state of flux.

The weekly model backtest failed, with an Acerbi-Szekely statistic of -0.6659. Interestingly, we recorded 0 VaR exceptions. This suggests that while our VaR model is not underestimating the frequency of extreme events, the Expected Shortfall estimate itself might be too conservative, or the specific tail events are not being captured effectively by the Acerbi-Szekely test. I will be looking into the composition of the tail events to understand this better.
